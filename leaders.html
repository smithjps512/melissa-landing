Banning AI doesn't keep students safe. Teaching it well does.

Students already use AI at home and on their phones. A ban at school just means they learn it with no rules and no adult in the room. In Stop Banning What You Should Be Teaching, James Smith shows schools how to teach AI with clear rules for every assignment. Melissa is built on that approach, with student data protected and the AI kept on a short leash.

Student data

"Is our students' data safe?"

Will student data be used to train AI?

No. Student data is never used to train AI models, never sold and never used for advertising. It is used only to run Melissa for the class it belongs to.

Does Melissa meet FERPA and COPPA?

Melissa acts as a school official with a legitimate educational interest under FERPA, so student records are used only to provide the service and are not shared without authorization. For students under 13, Melissa relies on the COPPA school-consent exception and collects only the minimum data the class needs.

What do you collect about students?

Names, class membership, the work they submit and their progress. Students join with a class code and need no email address. Class Pulse feedback and the Quick Clash review game need no student login at all.

How is it protected, and where does it live?

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and each teacher's classes are kept separate from everyone else's. It is stored in the United States.

Who else handles our data?

A short list of service providers, each with a specific job:

  • Vercel hosts the app, and Supabase stores the data.
  • Anthropic (Claude) is the AI that writes lessons and feedback.
  • OpenAI draws illustrations from a short picture description, never from student names or records.
  • Pexels supplies stock photos for slides, found by lesson topic.
  • Stripe handles teacher payments only.

Will you sign our data agreement?

A data processing agreement is available for districts on request. Write to hello@melissaforeducators.ai. In a district adoption, the agreement is signed before any student data is loaded.

What the AI sees

"What does the AI know about my students?"

Does the AI see student names?

Lessons, slides, puzzles and games are created without student names or personal details. Melissa's instructions to the AI say so on every one.

What about IEPs, 504 plans and special education?

Melissa never reads, stores or writes an IEP or a 504 plan. Teachers pick from seven plain accommodations, like extended time or fewer items, and the AI sees only a general instruction such as "reduce the number of items." A teacher's notes and a student's diagnosis never leave Melissa.

How does personalization work?

Melissa rewrites a student's copy of an assignment around their interests, like soccer or space, while keeping the same skills and difficulty. Teachers can turn personalization off for any student.

Are AI images safe for school?

Generated illustrations never show real or identifiable people, carry no text, and requests that conflict with these rules are declined. Teachers review images before using them.

Cheating and learning

"Won't students just let AI do the work?"

How do students know when AI is allowed?

Every assignment carries one of three zones: AI Prohibited when the process is the learning, AI Assisted when AI can help but the student's thinking leads, and AI Required when the goal is learning to work with AI. Students see the zone at the top of the assignment, and Melissa follows it too.

What does "AI Assisted" actually mean?

Melissa gives hints and questions, not answers. Teachers can also add checks that the work is the student's own, like a short oral defense, a reflection or peer review.

Who decides grades?

Teachers. Simple practice questions are scored instantly so students get quick feedback, but nothing counts until the teacher approves it. In Student Projects, teachers see how each student worked with AI, and working with it well is part of the grade.

Student safety

"What if a student is in trouble?"

What does Melissa do if a student says they're hurt or unsafe?

Melissa takes it seriously, encourages the student to talk with a trusted adult right away, like a parent, teacher or counselor, and shares the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. It never promises to keep it secret. In Student Projects, Melissa also flags the concern to the teacher.

Is Melissa a general chatbot?

No. Students use Melissa inside the assignments and projects their teacher sets, under that assignment's AI zone. It is a teaching tool, not social media and not an open chat app.

Your network

"Will it work on our network?"

What does IT need to do?

Allow a short list of domains over HTTPS and categorize Melissa as Education, not as AI or chat. It runs in the browser, with no software, extensions or student email accounts. The full list is on our page for IT administrators, and your team can check a school device with our network test.
