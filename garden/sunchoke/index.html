Sunchoke (Jerusalem Artichoke)

Helianthus tuberosus

Plant & Info Care Instructions

About this plant

  • Sunchokes are resilient, high-yielding perennial sunflowers native to North America.
  • The above-ground plant looks like a wild sunflower and easily grows 6 to 14 feet tall — tall enough to shade nearby crops.
  • They spread rapidly through underground rhizomes. Any tiny piece of tuber left in the soil grows into a new plant next spring.
  • The edible parts are the knobby underground tubers, which look like ginger root. One plant can yield over a gallon of tubers.
  • They need a long season — 120 to 150 days — and bloom bright yellow late, in August or September.
  • They store energy as inulin, not starch. Gut bacteria love it, which is why sunchokes are famous for causing gas.
  • Frost makes them sweeter: it breaks inulin down into fructose for a nuttier flavor.
  • Raw, they are crisp and sweet like water chestnuts. Cooked, they turn creamy like a potato.
